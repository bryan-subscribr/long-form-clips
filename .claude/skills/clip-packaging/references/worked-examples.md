## Example 6 — Hot-seat answer, short (3:28): the jewelry clip

Source clip: MoreMozi `Fsp8Zdid4eE` (3:28, published 2026-09-21). Channel's actual package: title `You Need to Sell Stories.` · thumb **Nobody Buys Spreadsheets** (2.3K views in first day).

## 1. Transcript (from `scripts/transcribe.py`, 105 segments, 776 words)

Opens on a hot-seat asker: "Partnered with my wife on her business, we sell jewelry with purpose, natural stones. What would be the best content to post for advertising? We use TikTok." Alex: TikTok Shop, aggressive affiliate commissions, collab posts (0:24–0:58). Then the core (0:59–1:33): "the big thing … I wanna tell the narrative, the story behind the jewelry … everyone has T-shirts, there's nothing sexy about a T-shirt … pair this commodity of jewelry with the meaning that you then ascribe to it." Proof story (1:35–2:03): sold "millions of dollars of hats," people paid "$100 essentially for a hat" after "four or five minutes … why I wear this hat." Application (2:05–2:28): show unboxing, "where was the stone sourced, why did you design it that way." Closing principle (2:26–3:27): "don't be afraid to repeat stuff … you need significantly less variety in your content than you think you do … Dave Ramsey … 35, 40 years … spend less than you make … the variety will just come from the examples."

## 2. Classification

Hot-seat / audience question (asker self-intro + question in first 20s). Default archetype: quoted first-person question, with a statement alternative because the answer contains two clean claims.

## 3. Raw materials

1. Asked question: *"We sell jewelry with purpose. What would be the best content to post for advertising?"* Persona: husband-and-wife brand, TikTok.
2. Core claim: pair the commodity with a story; and you need far less variety than you think.
3. ## What the example teaches

- The channel chose the shortest speaker-frame verdict for the title and invented an opposite for the thumb. That is the `verdict → sharper verdict` pairing.
- The number line ($100 hat) was left unused by the channel. On a clip with a spoken number like this, the number thumb is usually the safer pick because it is verifiable inside the clip.
- Note the transcript's first two seconds are a false start ("Partnered with my, okay, wait a minute") — the editors kept the fumble. The hook is the asker's question, not the fumble; title from the question or the answer, never from the fumble.

## Cross-example patterns

1. The channel's winning thumbs were the *first spoken line* (Just Start.), a *number from the middle* (17 hour work days, 45 Zoom hours), or a *slogan made from the constraint* (Two hands. No excuses.). Look in those three places first.
2. When a clip produced two or three strong stingers, the channel made two or three uploads over months, each with a different thumb line. Bank the unused stingers in the ledger.
3. The two underperformers here (Dog Launch 20K on brand strength, Three Generations 4K) both had a title that did not describe what the clip argues. Step 3 (write the core claim before choosing the archetype) is the fix.
4. Every hypothetical prompt from a host is an `If I Wanted…`/`How I'd…` title with the timeframe converted to the channel's tokens (*3 months* → *90 Days*).
